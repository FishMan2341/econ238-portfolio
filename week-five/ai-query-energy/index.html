How Much Power Is an AI Query?

One chatbot prompt uses about as much electricity as a Google search did in 2009. One proposed AI data center about 35 miles from Rochester would draw as much power as about 420,000 homes. Both statements are true. This exhibit shows how they fit together.

Hover or tap a bar for its source. Microwave (1,000 W), LED bulb (10 W) and gas-car (25 mpg × 33.7 kWh per gallon of gasoline) values are simple arithmetic from the stated assumptions. AI values cover running the model (inference), not training it.

What jumps out: a text prompt is in the same range as a web search. Generating an image is a few prompts. A long-document prompt is closer to half an hour of streaming. Five seconds of AI video is about a third of a mile of driving. "AI" is not one number. What you ask for matters more than whether you used AI.

Read this critically: the two lowest numbers come from the companies themselves and haven't been independently audited. Google also reports that its per-prompt energy fell 33x in one year (May 2024 to May 2025). That shows how fast these figures go stale and why older citations mislead. Model size matters too: MIT Technology Review measured an open-source 405-billion-parameter model at about 1.9 Wh per answer, roughly 60x a small 8-billion-parameter model.

U.S. data center electricity (TWh/year)

Lawrence Berkeley National Lab (Dec 2024). 2023 = 176 TWh, 4.4% of U.S. electricity. The 2028 bars show the low and high projections (325–580 TWh; 6.7–12%). Globally, the IEA estimates 415 TWh in 2024, about 1.5% of world electricity, roughly doubling by 2030.

Same units, very different sizes (TWh/year)

Log scale. "All ChatGPT prompts" = 2.5 billion prompts/day (OpenAI, July 2025) × 0.34 Wh × 365 ≈ 0.31 TWh, an average draw of about 35 MW. STAMP at full load = 500 MW × 8,760 hours = 4.38 TWh.

The puzzle this reveals: by these numbers, every ChatGPT text prompt on Earth uses about one-fourteenth of what the single proposed STAMP data center could draw, and less than 0.2% of U.S. data center electricity. So per-prompt chat is not where most of the power goes. The rest is training new models, longer "reasoning" and agent tasks, image and video, enterprise and cloud computing, and capacity built ahead of demand. When someone says "your chatbot habit is using up the grid," the numbers don't support it. When someone says "AI's energy use is trivial," the data center build-out doesn't support that either.
Exhibit 5 · Close to home

The STAMP data center: what 500 MW means for Western New York

Stream U.S. Data Centers proposed a three-building campus at the STAMP industrial park in the Town of Alabama, Genesee County, about halfway between Buffalo and Rochester. It sits next to the Tonawanda Seneca Nation.

Where it stands (as of mid-2026)

  • 2025: The Tonawanda Seneca Nation and the Sierra Club sued over environmental review. The county economic development agency rescinded an earlier approval, and Stream re-proposed the project.
  • July 14, 2026: A new lawsuit challenges the 2012 zoning agreement that underpins all of STAMP.
  • July 15, 2026: Governor Hochul ordered a statewide pause of up to one year on new large data center approvals while the state studies impacts. She also proposed ending data center tax subsidies. The project's future is uncertain.
The economics question: the per-prompt numbers above are small private costs. The real policy questions are about the facility: who pays for new grid capacity, what the local spillovers are (land, runoff, noise, light, effects on the Nation's adjacent lands), and whether $1.46 billion in public subsidy for 125 jobs is a good trade. Those are classic externality and public-finance questions, and none of them shows up in "Wh per query."
So what?

What this shows, and what it doesn't

1
Per prompt, text AI is small.

Roughly a web search, or a second of a microwave, by current disclosures.

2
"AI" is not one number.

Long documents, big models, images and especially video can cost 100x to 1,000x+ a simple prompt.

3
Old numbers stick around.

The 2023 3 Wh estimate still circulates even though efficiency improved about 10x. Check the date on any energy claim.

4
In aggregate, AI is big, and growing fast.

U.S. data centers may go from 4.4% to as much as 12% of national electricity by 2028. A single proposed facility near Rochester would match hundreds of thousands of homes.

5
The policy fight is local.

It's about specific facilities, grids, subsidies and neighbors, not individual users' prompts.

Not claimed here: that AI's environmental footprint is negligible, or that it's catastrophic. Training energy, water, carbon intensity of the local grid, and hardware manufacturing are left out, and each could be its own exhibit.

How this was made

Methods, AI use, and how to extend this

What I asked the AI (Claude) to do

  • Find every published per-prompt energy estimate, with dates and assumptions, and explain why they differ. I pushed it to separate company-reported from independent numbers.
  • Find comparable energy figures for everyday activities from primary sources (IEA, EIA, Google), and for the U.S. and global data center totals (LBNL, IEA).
  • Pull reporting on the STAMP project's size, subsidies, jobs and legal status as of mid-2026.
  • Do the unit conversions, write the calculator, and build the charts (Chart.js). Then I asked "does the aggregate math actually add up?" That's where the ChatGPT-vs-STAMP comparison came from.

Computations (all reproducible)

  • Joules to Wh: divide by 3,600 (e.g., 2,282 J image = 0.63 Wh).
  • All ChatGPT prompts: 2.5×109/day × 0.34 Wh × 365 = 0.31 TWh/yr; ÷ 8,760 h ≈ 35 MW average. The live counter at the top uses 2.5×109 ÷ 86,400 ≈ 28,900 prompts per second.
  • STAMP: 500 MW × 8,760 h = 4.38 TWh/yr (upper bound; real facilities run below 100%). ÷ 10,380 kWh per home ≈ 422,000 homes.
  • Car: 33.7 kWh per gallon (EPA's MPGe convention) ÷ 25 mpg = 1.35 kWh per mile.

The next questions someone could ask

  • Add training: how many prompts does it take to "pay off" the energy used to train a model?
  • Convert to carbon using the actual NYISO/Western NY grid mix instead of a national average. New York's upstate grid is mostly hydro and nuclear.
  • Do the same ladder for water (Google reports 0.26 mL per median Gemini prompt).
  • Compare STAMP's subsidy per job with other Western NY incentive deals.
