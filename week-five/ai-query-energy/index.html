How Much Power Is an AI Query?

One chatbot prompt uses about as much electricity as a Google search did in 2009. Yet U.S. data centers could go from 4.4% of the country's electricity to as much as 12% by 2028. Both are true. Here is how they fit together.

What this shows

A typical text prompt uses about 0.24–0.34 Wh, in the same range as a 2009 Google search and about one second of a microwave. But "AI" isn't one number: an image is about 2 prompts' worth, a book-length document about 120, and five seconds of AI video about 1,300.

Per prompt, chat is small. The real story is the total: U.S. data centers used 4.4% of the nation's electricity in 2023 and could reach 6.7–12% by 2028. Everyday chat prompts are only a small slice of that. What you ask AI to do matters more than whether you use it.

Caveat: the lowest per-prompt figures come from Google and OpenAI themselves and haven't been independently audited. The image and video numbers come from open-source models measured on specific hardware.

Google also reports that its per-prompt energy fell 33x in one year (May 2024 to May 2025), which shows how fast these figures go stale. Model size matters too: MIT Technology Review measured an open-source 405-billion-parameter model at about 1.9 Wh per answer, roughly 60x a small 8-billion-parameter model.

U.S. data center electricity (TWh/year)

Lawrence Berkeley National Lab (Dec 2024). 2023 = 176 TWh, 4.4% of U.S. electricity. 2028 bars show the low and high projections (325–580 TWh; 6.7–12%).

Same units, very different sizes (TWh/year)

Log scale. All ChatGPT prompts = 2.5 billion/day (OpenAI, July 2025) × 0.34 Wh × 365 ≈ 0.31 TWh. Global data centers: IEA, 415 TWh in 2024 (about 1.5% of world electricity).

The puzzle this reveals: a full year of every ChatGPT text prompt (~0.31 TWh) is less than 0.2% of U.S. data center electricity. So everyday chat isn't where most of the power goes. The rest is training new models, longer "reasoning" and agent tasks, image and video, enterprise and cloud computing, and capacity built ahead of demand. "Your chatbot habit is using up the grid" doesn't hold up, and neither does "AI's energy use is trivial."
All five takeaways (and what this doesn't claim)The full argument in five cards
1
Per prompt, text AI is small.

Roughly a web search, or a second of a microwave, by current disclosures.

2
"AI" is not one number.

Long documents, big models, images and especially video can cost 100x to 1,000x+ a simple prompt.

3
Old numbers stick around.

The 2023 3 Wh estimate still circulates even though efficiency improved about 10x. Check the date on any energy claim.

4
In aggregate, AI is big, and growing fast.

U.S. data centers may go from 4.4% to as much as 12% of national electricity by 2028.

5
The policy questions are about infrastructure.

Grid capacity, who pays for it, and where facilities are built, not individual users' prompts.

Not claimed here: that AI's environmental footprint is negligible, or that it's catastrophic. Training energy, water, the carbon intensity of the grid, and hardware manufacturing are left out, and each could be its own exhibit.

Methods, AI use, and how to extend thisWhat I asked the AI to do, the computations, and next questions

What I asked the AI (Claude) to do

  • Find every published per-prompt energy estimate, with dates and assumptions, and explain why they differ. I pushed it to separate company-reported from independent numbers.
  • Find comparable energy figures for everyday activities from primary sources (IEA, EIA, Google), and U.S. and global data center totals (LBNL, IEA).
  • Do the unit conversions, write the calculator, and build the charts (Chart.js). Then I asked "does the aggregate math actually add up?" That's where the comparison between all ChatGPT prompts and total data center use came from.

Computations (all reproducible)

  • Joules to Wh: divide by 3,600 (e.g., a 2,282 J image = 0.63 Wh).
  • All ChatGPT prompts: 2.5×109/day × 0.34 Wh × 365 = 0.31 TWh/yr. The live counter uses 2.5×109 ÷ 86,400 ≈ 28,900 prompts per second.
  • Share of U.S. data centers: 0.31 ÷ 176 TWh ≈ 0.18%.
  • Car: 33.7 kWh per gallon (EPA's MPGe convention) ÷ 25 mpg = 1.35 kWh per mile.

The next questions someone could ask

  • Add training: how many prompts does it take to "pay off" the energy used to train a model?
  • Convert to carbon using the actual New York grid mix instead of a national average. Upstate New York's grid is mostly hydro and nuclear.
  • Do the same ladder for water (Google reports 0.26 mL per median Gemini prompt).
  • Compare data centers with other sources of electricity growth. The IEA expects them to be only about a tenth of global demand growth to 2030, behind air conditioning, motors and EVs.
