The Cows Were Counted First: London's Great Smog and the Price of a Warm Room

A 1952 London fog killed about 12,000 people, but the first recorded casualties were prize cattle. A lecture on coal, politics, and the price of a warm room.

What it's about

In December 1952, a temperature inversion trapped the smoke of millions of coal fires over London for five days. Prize cattle at the Smithfield Show were examined and reported within days; the human death toll was undercounted for half a century. The lecture follows postwar coal exports and "nutty slack," Harold Macmillan's housing drive, Sir Hugh Beaver's committee, and the Clean Air Act of 1956, then asks what actually cleared the air and why the hearth has returned as a luxury today.

Structured after Arnold Kling's "AIDS and a Blue Dress," which pairs two documents a decade apart; this lecture pairs two documents sixty-eight years apart.
